# Primary production and agriculture runtime review

Reviewed 24 November 2026 against engine revision `f01273ca2`. This record follows production from the player or script entry point to its output and saved state. It records the subsequent engine repairs separately from world design choices.

## What owns each resource

| Scenario | Owner and handoff |
| --- | --- |
| A gatherer searches for herbs, a meal, sticks or stones | The effective foragable profile defines possible results and pool limits. Each room owns its remaining stock. A successful search places items or a material commodity into play. Forager diets can draw on the same named pool. |
| A hunter brings home a carcass | A race butchery profile maps surviving anatomy to products. Skinning yields pelts, named subcategories can yield trophies, and final breakdown yields the remaining authored primals. A craft can then turn those items into cuts or processed goods. |
| A farm supplies food or wood | A field owns crop, orchard, pasture or woodland state. Apiaries are adjuncts and field herds are counted livestock. Weather, season, field condition, livestock and pollination affect production. Property governs who may initiate work. A local project supplies labour and materials. |
| Workers prospect, quarry and process stone | Room tags describe a site. A discovery action can place a marker and a commodity output action can create a pile. A local project owns the persistent work. A craft can dress an individual batch. A finite ore reserve and the direction and ownership of a new shaft need authored state. |
| An expedition plunders a ruin | The loot table chooses and creates the package. The ruin, container or dungeon instance owns the opportunity to claim it. A successful empty roll can consume that opportunity just as a valuable find can. |

**Additional systems to include.** Item prototypes, solid materials, commodity mass and secondary tags are the common vocabulary between these systems. A pelt or primal item can carry an identity that a recipe needs. A commodity pile can be divided by mass and distinguished by a tag such as seed stock or raw ore. Race diets, wildlife AI and spawners own the living hunting population. Field herds represent headcount and condition separately from live NPCs with bodies. Ordinary project labour, optional payment and employment supply workers. Room overlays, FutureProg registers and activation hooks belong in the advanced mine and ruin examples. The existing craft and project guides cover the editors.

## Individual gathering and wildlife

The `foragable` editor defines a result, and `foragableprofile` collects results and defines maximum and hourly recovery by yield name. Resolution checks the room's explicit profile, its zone and then the current overlay's terrain. A shared profile gives rooms the same rules. Their saved balances are independent.

At the end of the timed `forage` action the command checks the effective profile and the character's check outcome. An untargeted search chooses among eligible results by relative weight. The item quantity or commodity weight expression determines output. A successful output debits the **searched pool once**, by the result's configured percentage of one yield point. A 25% cost needs 0.25 points even if the output is a stack. A zero-cost result needs the configured key. The post-forage prog is called for the generated item objects. [Player command](../../MudSharpCore/Commands/Modules/GameModule.cs) · [Profile selection](../../MudSharpCore/Work/Foraging/ForagableProfile.cs)

Room saves persist per-key balances in `RoomsForagableYields`. Loading restores those balances and reconciles them with the effective profile. New keys start full, surviving keys are capped to the new maximum and retired keys are removed. Depleted rooms subscribe to the engine's hourly heartbeat. Each tick restores the configured hourly amount up to capacity, subject to any environmental magic recovery penalty. The load path does not award missed hourly ticks during downtime. [Room yield lifecycle](../../MudSharpCore/Construction/Room.cs)

For the forest-management example, give two reachable clearings the same vegetation yield and a grazing race that eats it. A hungry animal can take a fractional bite directly. A wildlife group that cannot feed locally and has an urgent need can select a reachable preferred neighbour with a food opportunity. Its recent-forage memory, other food or water, and movement policy also affect the choice. Leaving browse in one clearing can influence a hunt. The AI does not survey the forest and choose the largest balance. Field woodland yield and room forage stock have separate owners. Cutting timber does not automatically change the herd's browse. ## From raw materials to production

The field operation and the craft input are two distinct ways to draw on cultivated land. A crop or orchard harvest releases authored commodity outputs scaled by health, yield potential, work outcome and configured base mass. An annual harvest clears the crop. A perennial orchard retains it for the next cycle. Woodland operations can release materials and charge a configured yield cost. Field herds consume pasture and can produce secondary commodities proportional to headcount. An apiary stores its own yield and can also support pollination nearby. Terrain defaults supply starting settings when a field is created. They do not create a field in every room of that terrain. [Agriculture runtime](Agriculture_Runtime_Model.md)

A short individual activity such as gathering weaving rods from managed coppice can use the craft `field` input. Scouting checks the actor's property access, field use, optional crop or woodland definition, and minimum condition, health and yield. At reservation the input can spend crop or woodland yield, and its saved input data records the field ID, quality and consumed amount. The debit happens before the finished product is released. For a subsequent batch such as dressing quarried blocks, match the incoming commodity by material and any pile tag, reserve the required mass and author the normal and failed products in the craft. A fixed output needs no script. [Field input](../../MudSharpCore/Work/Crafts/Inputs/AgricultureFieldInput.cs) · [Craft runtime](../Crafting/Crafting_System_Runtime_and_Extensibility.md)

Seed requirements displayed on a crop are descriptive metadata. The sowing project's material requirement controls what is actually supplied. Stock sowing uses a material tag and the secondary seed-stock tag. It can therefore accept seed of another qualifying material regardless of the chosen crop. Crop-specific seed consumption is an authoring limitation of the shared template. Ordinary project progress, payment rates and the work site are saved on the active project. Agriculture separately saves the selected field, target and worker skill contribution against the active project ID. [Stock seed requirement](../../DatabaseSeeder/Seeders/AgricultureSeeder/AgricultureSeeder.cs) · [Active project](../../MudSharpCore/Work/Projects/ConcreteTypes/ActiveProject.cs)

## Defect resolutions

| Workflow | Engine behaviour after repair |
| --- | --- |
| Inherited grazing diet | Bite size and nutrition both use the effective list of edible forage yields, including yields defined only by a parent race. |
| Starting field work | The ordinary project start checks all phases for an agriculture completion action and directs players to `field start`. Starting through a field still enforces the template's initiation policy. The field context and active project are inserted together. |
| Prospecting samples | The stock iron, tin, copper and lead samples require successful placement of the matching new marker earlier in the same phase. The ordinary commodity action can still produce output independently. |
| Scripted farming | `StartFieldProject` returns false if the actor is away from the supplied field. A successful start uses the actor's local work site in that field's room. |

## Boundaries for the mine and ruin examples

A mine entrance can own a requested cardinal direction and the project that has reserved it. Persist a text direction for an unopened shaft. An exit object cannot be stored in the variable register, and an unopened shaft has no exit yet. Initiation and its explanation take a character. The start, cancel and finish progs and a phase prog action receive the active project. Snapshot the direction for that project, check both the reservation and current exits again at completion, and release only the reservation it owns. The project location remains the work site if the initiator travels. Project lifecycle callbacks suffice for this workflow. An interaction hook is useful if choosing the direction is part of using a fixture.

Room expansion requires an editable overlay package. `CreateRoom` creates the new room, `LinkCells` checks both ends and adds the reciprocal connection, and approval and swapping activate the overlays. These are separate operations. Link failure after creation can leave a room in the draft, and a phase prog's return cannot veto phase completion. Building a new shaft, assigning a deposit and accounting for a finite reserve are authored operations. The native discovery action checks the room tag and suppresses duplicate markers at the site. The native commodity action produces its configured mass each time it completes. It has no ore balance to spend. [Project runtime](../Crafting/Projects_System_Overview.md) · [Room creation](../../MudSharpCore/FutureProg/Functions/Location/CreateRoom.cs) · [Reciprocal exits](../../MudSharpCore/FutureProg/Functions/Location/Exits/LinkRooms.cs) · [Register storage](../../MudSharpCore/FutureProg/VariableRegister.cs)

For ruins, choose the lifetime of the claim explicitly. A persistent room can represent one opportunity in the world, a chest one opportunity per object, and a generated dungeon one opportunity per instance. A repeated deterministic seed reproduces a plan and can create another package. Use the caller's claim state and the materialiser's receipt to decide whether an opportunity has been spent. Writing a register and committing loot are separate persistence operations. Exactly-once recovery across a crash between them would require a durable claim tied to the load. ## Verification scope

The review traced command, FutureProg, AI, project action and persistence paths. The focused core test run covered foraging, butchery helpers, loot definitions, agriculture authoring and operations, native yield accounting, discovery, commodity output and local work sites. It passed 181 tests. That run preceded the fixes. After the repairs, 169 focused core tests passed. They cover inherited bite size and nutrition, catalogue refusal, onsite field-context persistence, remote-start rejection and prospecting outcomes for new, legacy and custom projects. The review and repair tests did not simulate a server crash or run a complete shaft-building or dungeon-claim scenario. Butchery also lacks the loot materialiser's staged rollback if an exception occurs partway through creating several products. Partial output on that exceptional path remains an unverified risk, distinct from ordinary cancellation before completion.
