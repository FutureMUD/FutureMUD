# Seeded clan forms of address

This review covers the 50 non-Catholic templates in `ClanSeeder.cs` and `ClanSeeder.Templates.cs`. The Catholic suite has its own address content. All rules use the generic [clan forms-of-address system](../Economy/Clan_Forms_of_Address_Design.md); this change adds no engine commands, comparison policies or schema.

## Authoring policy

`ClanSeeder.Addresses.cs` contains explicit owner-to-address mappings. It does not convert arbitrary rank names, job descriptions or abbreviations into honorifics. Plain styles require one Any rule; styles following the existing feminine/masculine title conventions use an `IsFemale` rule followed by an unconditional default. Modern military officer styles use Lower-only `IsFemale`/`IsMale` rules for Ma'am/Sir, followed by the normal rank/office default. The shared predicates accept character/chargen input, so installation checks signature compatibility rather than requiring a character-only signature. These predicates are global utility progs and remain shared when cloning.

As agreed, modern military commissioned/warrant officers receive Sir/Ma'am from lower-ranking members, with common shortened rank-based English address for peers and superiors. Outsiders, null speakers and subjects matching neither gender predicate use the rank/office default. NCOs retain rank address. These relational rules apply to Army, Infantry Company, Battalion, Naval Organisation, Capital Ship and Air Force; historical Age of Sail and mercenary templates keep their separately authored styles. Selected appointments override rank with explicit address priorities: a ship's Captain, a First Sergeant appointment, and royal or civic offices. Staff billets usually leave rank address intact. Generic occupations, membership status and family seniority acquire no automatic honorific.

Most selected courtesy styles are explicitly public. Gang and intelligence-agency styles are members-only, so an outsider/null-speaker lookup cannot reveal them. Normal identity and visibility checks still apply. Public addresses do not enable outsider full-look hints.

These are editable gameplay defaults, not claims of one protocol spanning every country, regiment, historical period or religious tradition. Forms are standalone vocatives, without automatic names.

## Agreed decisions and remaining ambiguities

1. **Military etiquette — agreed:** Sir/Ma'am from lower ranks; rank/office forms for peers, superiors and outsiders. The broad Army template remains a hybrid; specialised warrant-officer and regimental usages can still be tailored to a chosen service tradition. The Navy organisation's CO is not necessarily a vessel's captain.
2. **Religion and traditional institutions:** the legacy monastic template is intentionally left unset, as agreed. For the other broad religious/court templates, region, period and language remain open choices. No new ordination or kinship inference is introduced here.
3. **Roman address - agreed:** Latin direct-address forms. The distinctions and remaining source ambiguities are recorded below; no blanket Domine or late-imperial court honorific is inferred.
4. **Institutional preference:** decide whether a particular company, party, union, hospital or clandestine organisation wants office labels, personal names, Comrade, Doctor, Sir/Ma'am or another convention. Clinician and Medical Officer do not establish a doctor's qualification.
5. **Existing installations — agreed:** only newly seeded templates receive these defaults. Older non-Catholic templates have no explicit stock-ownership marker and are preserved, including empty lists; a matching name does not establish ownership.

## Roman and peerage authoring decisions

Roman forms are explicit grammatical role vocatives based on classical Latin, rather than a claim that one surviving regulation prescribed this address table. Second-declension forms include Tribunus -> Tribune, Praefectus -> Praefecte and Legatus -> Legate; common role words Tesserarius and Vexillarius give Tesserarie and Vexillarie. Third-declension forms such as Centurio, Consul and Sacerdos remain unchanged. The forms omit macrons and an optional O. No declension algorithm or historical policy is added to the engine.

The legion describes itself as post-Marian. Its existing labels mix branches and contain spelling/qualification inconsistencies, so addresses generally use shortened roles. Legio and Immunes are collective labels; Sesquiplicarius is a pay distinction. These and basic status rows remain unset. No Imperator is inferred from the legate rank.

The two auxiliary staff ranks are corrected at their source on fresh installation: Beneficarius Cohortis/Alae -> Beneficiarius Cohortis/Alae, title Beneficiarius, abbreviation Bnf, address Beneficiarie. Their copied Centurio titles/abbreviations were inconsistent with both the named staff role and the existing Caligatius Beneficiarius paygrade. The corrected spelling also restores the existing name-based paygrade links, which previously matched neither rank. Their order, rank paths, privileges and compensation amounts are preserved; this is not a wider historical hierarchy redesign. Existing template IDs, rank names, customisations and live clans are not rewritten on rerun, in keeping with the agreed fresh-template-only scope.

The city has no precise period and already allows feminine civic titles. Address variants Senatrix, Proquaestrix, Propraetrix, Quaestrix, Praetrix and Dictatrix follow that existing fictional convention; they are not presented as evidence of women holding those classical offices. Existing membership rules and title progs are unchanged. Patricius, Eques and citizenship/freedom labels are not treated as personal honorifics. Appointment priorities choose one style when offices stack: Dictator, Consul, Censor, Princeps Senatus, Praetor, Tribunus Plebis, Aedilis, Quaestor, in that order. This is a gameplay display preference, not a reconstruction of Roman constitutional precedence.

The cult remains generic. Sacerdos is usable for a priest or priestess; a generic chief pontiff receives Pontifex without assuming the specific Roman state office Pontifex Maximus. Its office styles prefer Chief Pontiff over Augur over Temple Priest. This does not infer ordination or alter Catholic content.

The peerage review confirms that all clearly titled grades already have defaults, including baronets and the intermediate peerage grades. Untitled gentility is left unset: neither Esquire nor the broad Noble grade establishes Sir/Dame or My Lord/My Lady. Chivalric preliminary grades also receive no knighthood style. No additional British courtesy rule is imposed on the separate regional court templates.

## Installation and reruns

The seeder captures existing clan IDs before creating missing templates, then installs these rules only on the new template identities from that invocation. Existing templates, renamed stock, live clans, custom addresses, deliberately cleared lists and address priorities are preserved on rerun. A stock-name collision is not adopted. Newly installed missing templates still receive their styles even when other templates already exist.

The existing `Additive / InstallMissing` contract remains unchanged: no new question, replay profile, migration or engine policy is introduced. Existing worlds can use ordinary clan-building commands to set addresses, or clone a deliberately maintained template. This content does not automatically upgrade existing live clans.

## Source notes

- [US Army Regulation 600–20, table 1–1](https://home.army.mil/cavazos/4417/2115/0984/AR_600-20_Army_Command_Policy.pdf) supplies the Army grade/address distinction used for the operational templates. The hybrid broad Army template is an explicit adaptation.
- [Canadian Armed Forces heritage manual, chapter 11](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/defence/caf/military-identity-system/heritage-manual/chapter-11.html) illustrates differences between formal rank/appointment address and relational Sir/Ma'am usage; it is not applied as a universal service policy.
- [US Navy NAVSEA employee handbook](https://www.navsea.navy.mil/portals/103/documents/nswc_carderock/newemployeehandbook.pdf) describes vessel commanding officers as Captain and distinguishes enlisted rates and officer/billet address. [US government forms of address](https://highways.dot.gov/laws-regulations/directives/orders/correspondence-manual/appendix-c-external-and-internal-forms) supports shortened officer forms. [Air Force Handbook 1 (2025)](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/af_a1/publication/afh1/afh1.pdf) supplies the enlisted forms, including Sergeant as an accepted senior-master-sergeant form and Chief for a chief master sergeant; [Air Force protocol guidance](https://www.jbcharleston.jb.mil/News/Commentaries/Display/Article/237944/joint-base-protocol-tip-of-the-week/) also notes service/rating differences.
- [Victoria's official protocol guidance](https://www.vic.gov.au/how-to-address-royalty-and-officials) distinguishes royal, noble and civic direct address. [Debrett's](https://debretts.com/etiquette-category/forms-of-address/) distinguishes a baronetess in her own right from a baronet's wife; the seeded female rank is the holder, not a spouse.
- [Roman Inscriptions of Britain, glossary](https://romaninscriptionsofbritain.org/reference/glossary) identifies beneficiarius as a soldier seconded for special duties; [Segedunum Roman Fort](https://www.northeastmuseums.org.uk/segedunum/learning/jobs-within-the-auxiliary-unit) lists the role on the commanding officer's staff separately from the centurion. Together with the template's existing dedicated paygrade, these support correcting the copied Centurio labels rather than preserving them. Beneficiarie is the regular authored vocative, not an independently attested etiquette formula.
- [Allen and Greenough on the vocative](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/grammar/latin/vocative), [second-declension forms](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/grammar/latin/2nd-declension-stem-paradigm-and-gender) and [case-form exceptions](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/grammar/latin/2nd-declension-case-forms) support the grammar used for Latin role address, including the distinction between proper names in -ius and adjectival forms in -ius. [The National Archives noun reference](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/latin/stage-1-latin/resources/stage-1-latin-grammar-resource/nouns/) also explains unchanged vocatives outside the second-declension exception. [Lewis and Short, Sacerdos](https://logeion.org/sacerdos) supplies priest/priestess; [Pontifex](https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/dictionaries/entry/urn%3Acite2%3Ascaife-viewer%3Adictionary-entries.atlas_v1%3Alat.ls.perseus-eng2-n37018/) distinguishes a pontiff from the Pontifex Maximus. These lexical and grammatical sources support an authored gameplay adaptation, not an attested etiquette code.
- [Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of America](https://www.goarch.org/epvisit2021/salutations) and [Serbian Orthodox parish guidance](https://hthspc.org/church-etiquette) demonstrate that monastic address conventions cannot simply be assumed identical across communities. The older mixed template therefore remains undecided.

The mapping review is source-based. Functional installation/rerun and native clone tests verify persistence, preservation, shared-predicate compatibility and execution; catalogue names, totals and authored wording are not frozen as test snapshots.
