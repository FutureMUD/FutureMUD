The following live descriptions are supplied for the restored Antiquity identities. They replace only the managed toolkit prose, not the archived source definitions.

### Achaean

The Achaeans are Greek-speaking people of the towns and farms of Achaea. Their households share the speech of the markets and assemblies while preserving the customs of their own cities.

**Author-only mapping note:** The shared Koine is the toolkit default for this broad Achaean identity; no separate Achaean language entity is invented.

### Aeolian

The Aeolians are Greek people of Lesbos, the Aeolian coast and kindred communities. Their speech and songs preserve the traditions of their cities and ancestral households.

**Author-only mapping note:** Reuse the retained Aeolic Greek entity.

### Ionian

The Ionians are Greek people of the Aegean islands and the Ionian coast. Their towns are bound together by sea travel, trade, shared sanctuaries and the Ionian tongue.

**Author-only mapping note:** Reuse the retained Ionic Greek entity.

### Dorian

The Dorians are Greek people whose communities stretch across the Peloponnese, the islands and the western colonies. Their households preserve the Doric tongue and the customs of their cities.

**Author-only mapping note:** Reuse the retained Doric Greek entity.

### Italiote

The Italiotes are Greek-speaking people of the cities of southern Italia. Their families maintain Greek speech, festivals and learning amid close dealings with their Italic neighbours.

**Author-only mapping note:** Koine is a deliberate broad default for a mixed colonial identity, not a claim that every colonial dialect was identical.

### Cypriot

The Cypriots are people of Cyprus, where Greek-speaking towns and farming households prosper beside the sea. Their island connects the ports of the Aegean with the coasts of Syria and Egypt.

**Author-only mapping note:** Use Koine for the retained Greek Cypriot identity; do not invent an Arcado-Cypriot stage without a separate design.

### Cretan

The Cretans are Greek people of the cities, uplands and coastal settlements of Crete. Their Doric speech and island customs distinguish them among the peoples of the Aegean.

**Author-only mapping note:** Reuse retained Doric Greek; this is the explicit broad-era Cretan default.

### Anatolian

The Anatolians of the Greek-speaking cities of Asia Minor draw their public identity from their towns and civic traditions. Greek connects their households, markets and assemblies across the region.

**Author-only mapping note:** This exact source ethnicity describes Hellenised civic communities, not all Anatolian-language speakers. Do not map it to the unrelated broad language label Anatolian.

### Punic

The Punic people are the western Phoenician communities of Carthage and the surrounding coasts. Their households preserve the Punic tongue and traditions of seafaring, trade and cultivation.

**Author-only mapping note:** Punic uses the retained Phoenician skill as an explicit language-level abstraction; no new synonym language.

### Libyan

The Libyans of the Punic coast belong to the settled communities around the trading towns east of Carthage. Punic speech binds their households to the ports, while inland kin and neighbours follow their own tongues and customs.

**Author-only mapping note:** Keeps the exact source record's Punic coastal meaning; does not identify every indigenous Libyan as Phoenician-speaking.

### Mauretanian

The Mauretanians are the peoples of the western North African kingdoms, from cultivated valleys to the mountain country. Their households preserve local speech and kinship ties while trading with the towns of the coast.

**Author-only mapping note:** Use retained Numidian as a broad ancient Libyco-Berber gameplay skill. This is not modern Tashelhit and not a demonstrated uniform ancient vernacular.

### Berber

The Berber peoples live among the mountains, plains and oases of North Africa. Their clans and settled households preserve local tongues and customs alongside the coastal cities.

**Author-only mapping note:** Same explicit ancient Libyco-Berber skill abstraction; do not default to a later Arabic or modern Amazigh stage.

### Arabian

The Arabians are the settled and pastoral peoples of Arabia and the lands around Nabatea. Their households and clans share Arabic speech along the caravan roads, oases and grazing country.

**Author-only mapping note:** Reuse canonical Old Arabic; political language Aramaic is not an automatic ethnic native grant.

## 5. Weighting and names in records

Original active weights are left intact. Editorial temporal extensions use 60; regional borrowing uses60; reconstructed entries use 40. These values are convenience weights, not empirical frequencies. Where every available form is reconstructed, equal 40 weights still form a valid uniform profile.

Keep a given name and an optional byname. Random byname count0 is valid, so a 20-given-name profile is playable without fabricating a hereditary-surname system. Existing compound names remain whole elements. The neutral shared profile remains separate from binary-gender suggestions.

## 6. Source use and limitations

The retained evidence snapshot records exactly what the previous dataset supplied; preservation is not a new independent verification of every original citation. The Finnish naming study distinguishes directly sourced Christian names from a reconstruction of the older system. Baltic regional name transcriptions often preserve the record's Latin, Low German, Swedish or Slavic spelling rather than a reconstructed spoken form. The newer Lithuanian language-commission articles support analogical reconstruction and describe later revival; they do not establish the proposed medieval female bearers.

The Old Prussian feminine core is a deliberately more speculative use of retained male-name shapes with cross-Baltic analogy. No claim is made that an -a suffix can be applied to arbitrary Prussian words or every male name. These exact forms are approved fictional content, not inputs to a productive morphology generator.

Source IDs and URLs are in `research/sources.json`; exact original record locators and editorial reasoning are in the naming JSON. No coding agent needs to rediscover or invent the delivered names.
