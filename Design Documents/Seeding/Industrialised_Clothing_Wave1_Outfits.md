# Industrialised clothing — Wave 1 proposed ensembles

These are complete garment-list proposals within the approved [Wave 1 inventory](./Industrialised_Clothing_Wave1_Inventory.md) scope, not yet runtime-validated outfits. The user accepted the counts and overall scope on 2026-09-03, with conventional colours moved to outfit defaults. They deliberately reuse ordinary pieces. Gate 3/5 must prove wear order, anatomy/fit, attachment and colour behaviour. Handheld tools, weapons and domain-owned PPE are not silently inserted as worn clothing.

`I/M/N/F` have the inventory's exact era meanings. `Garments` lists planning keys in proposed inner-to-outer order; `key@skin-slug` selects one listed skin brief, slugged to lowercase hyphenated words. An unqualified key selects the authored default, not a random skin. No item receives an unlisted skin. Lists do not promise that all garments can currently be worn together.

An unqualified key deliberately loads a complete standalone unskinned garment. It is a real outfit choice, not an unresolved skin selection or placeholder. The proving set must include outfits and crafted products using those unskinned defaults alongside skinned variants.

Palette rules are deterministic, overridable defaults for these example ensembles, not restrictions on variable-colour stock. `neutral`: cream underwear/foundations, grey principal garments, brown footwear and leather accessories, cream remaining textile accessories. `blue`: cream underwear/foundations, blue principal garments, black footwear/leather accessories, blue other textiles. `earth`: cream underwear/foundations, brown principal garments/footwear, muted green other textiles. `formal`: cream underwear/foundations, dark grey principal garments, black footwear/leather accessories, cream shirt and black neckwear. `mourning`: cream hidden underlayers and black visible garments/accessories. `white`: white textiles and brown footwear. `saffron`: saffron robes with cream hidden underlayers and brown footwear. `maroon`: maroon robes with cream hidden underlayers and brown footwear. `sport`: cream hidden underlayers, blue principal garments, cream footwear and blue accessories. Material-native surfaces are not recoloured by prose; leather, fur, wood and metal selections must use truthful supported characteristics. Multi-colour skins retain their declared pattern while the palette controls their variable ground colour.

There are no approved fixed-colour exceptions in Wave 1. Resolve a valid explicit outfit-instance selection first, then the entry defaults below, then the ensemble palette. Reject unsupported values or unresolved colour channels before mutation; never silently substitute another colour. Defaults can be overridden coherently for another setting or institution without new base/skin identities, changing other outfits or changing ordinary stock. Persist resolved values so coordinated garments do not reroll on load. These rules reserve selection intent; typed characteristic IDs/load arguments and runtime proof are Wave 2 and later-gate work.

Explicit entry defaults, each local to the named outfit rather than its garments:

- `mourning_day`: black visible garments, including `bonnet@tonal-braid-trim` and `head_veil@tonal-border-embroidery`. These skins specify finishing, not a black colour lock or an assertion that all cultures mourn in black.
- `clerical_modern`: white `clerical_collar`, overriding the formal palette's black neckwear default. `preaching_service`: white `preaching_bands` and `attached_collar_shirt`, overriding its otherwise dark visible palette.
- `liturgical_service`, `liturgical_procession` and `liturgical_deacon`: black `cassock`; white `amice`, `alb`, `surplice` and `linen_cincture` wherever that entry occurs. Other vestments use the template's white palette, as one selected ceremony, not a permanent liturgical colour rule.
- `eastern_liturgy`: white `sticharion` and white principal vestments for this example. `eastern_clerical`: black `cassock`, `riassa` and `kamilavkion`.
- `convent_daily`: white `linen_wimple` beneath black `head_veil`; other visible textiles retain the template's black default.
- `jain_ascetic`: white `jain_robe`, `jain_shoulder_wrap` and `ascetic_mouthcloth`. `indic_prayer`: white `sacred_thread`, `dhoti` and `cotton_shawl`. `indic_outdoor`: white `dhoti`, `kurta` and `cotton_shawl`. These are particular example ensembles, not universal dress codes or eligibility rules.
- `zoroastrian_prayer`: white `sudreh`, `kusti`, `qamis` and `prayer_cap` through its white palette; neither the cap nor other base is colour-locked.
- `shrine_service`: white `joe_robe` and `shrine_hakama`, with black lacquer finish on `eboshi`. `shrine_attendant`: white `shrine_kosode`, red `shrine_red_hakama`. The latter is an explicit entry default overriding the white palette; its legacy colour-bearing source identity is preserved without becoming a colour restriction.
- `academic_ceremony`: blue hood lining against formal dark-grey outer cloth, a generic example rather than Oxford's exact degree specification.

For the reused two-channel petticoat and sarong, the defaults are the palette's principal textile colour for channel one and cream for channel two; use white and white in white ensembles. Other multicolour skins require explicit authored accent-channel defaults before Gate 3, not random choices at load time. Black lacquer and other finish selections must resolve to supported material-appropriate characteristics; documenting a conventional finish is not proof that the current binding supports it.
