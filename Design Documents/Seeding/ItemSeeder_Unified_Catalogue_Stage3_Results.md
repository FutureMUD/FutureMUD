# ItemSeeder stage 3: first explicit-stock batch

Historical checkpoint: subsequent completion work is recorded in the [completion results](ItemSeeder_Unified_Catalogue_Completion_Results.md). The statuses and measurements below describe this first batch, not the final migration.

Stage 3 has begun with 6,628 ordinary item definitions and 17 price-evidence records: all 178 Renaissance/Early Modern medical/repair items and all 6,450 industrial ordinary rows. This is the first batch, not completion of stage 3. Historical inline/spec/generated families remain to be converted. Recipes, specialised graphs, selection consolidation and real installer packaging remain their later stages.

## Prerequisite and implementation

The stage 2 measurements exposed both the C# string-literal limit and cold code-generation cost. This batch introduces a small public contract assembly, four industrial data assemblies and one medical data assembly. Content depends only on the contract and its shared domain types; it never references the installer. Installer adapters and ownership writers stay in DatabaseSeeder. Content is ordinary maintained C#; the one-time conversion script is developer evidence and is not called by a build or installation.

Records preserve the union of source fields. Industrial metadata retains domain, supported claims, source file/line/note, price-evidence IDs, craftability and lifecycle classification. Medical metadata retains categories and builder notes. Price records retain all original fields and decimal scale. Fixed variants are literal records; runtime binding remains only for the user's technology profile. Content-batch dispatch merely loads already-authored records.

The existing `EraMedicalRepairCatalogue` and `IndustrialisedItemCatalogue` shapes are temporary compatibility projections for validation/auditing, not additional authored sources. After unchanged whole-batch preflight and ordering, converted items go directly through the common typed writer so fields outside the old row shape are effective too. Industrial components retain profile resolution and skin permission combines authored permission with clothing requirements. Era/module context, first registration, customization and ownership remain with existing writers. Source notes retain the existing cleanup policy: catalogue notes are preserved in definitions/capture, seeded note text is removed from database builder notes, and builder-authored notes remain. Modern/Nuclear/Information are still non-selectable and the industrial food production-readiness failure remains enforced. Record-level physical readiness does not activate an era or override its programme gate.

The original item/price/medical TSVs and readers remain frozen differential oracles. `--legacy` capture uses those readers, including source-only medical categories. New definitions belong in `Catalogue/Assemblies/*/Content`; do not regenerate old TSVs to change live stock. The industrial audit uses the compiled document and hashes compiled source/project wiring too. Source fingerprints exclude nested build outputs and include all new project files. A recursive Roslyn guard covers every content subtree; compiled metadata checks require each content assembly's user-string heap to stay below 4 MiB.

The executable-only stock measurement is reproducible on an appropriate host:

```powershell
pwsh -NoProfile -File scripts/measure-compiled-item-stock.ps1 -Iterations 5 -OutputDirectory .artifacts/stock-publication
```

It publishes the actual seeder as framework-dependent, single-file, untrimmed IL and ReadyToRun executables, copies only each executable to separate temporary directories outside the repository, and alternates compiled versus legacy stock loading. It checks counts, matching full-value digests, stable binaries and native headers. It measures stock loading and compatibility projection, not database writes or full installer bootstrap. Process time includes output-digest serialization; publish timings use sequential cache states. Linux and ARM execution require their own hosts and do not become qualified through a Windows run.
