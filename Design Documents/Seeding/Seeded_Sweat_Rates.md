# Seeded whole-body sweat rates

20 October 2026. Common seed-data correction based on master `97c02768d`. Runtime remains litres per real minute. Breathing, ages, naming, runtime physiology, migrations and publication are outside this change.

## Consumer and effects

`MudSharpCore/Body/Implementations/BodyMovement.cs`, `StaminaMinuteHeartbeat`: an active, living body with a non-null sweat liquid and long-term exertion above Normal loses `base * multiplier` litres per real minute. Heavy uses 0.4, VeryHeavy 0.7, ExtremelyHeavy 0.9. Any other qualifying enum value takes the default 0.4. Multiply the base L/min by **24, 42 or 54** for steady-state L/real hour. Normal and below produce no sweat here. This is an exertion proxy: neither ambient heat nor individual acclimation/body mass continuously adjusts these rates. Starting the stamina callbacks can invoke an immediate extra tick; hourly tables assume 60 steady ticks, not an assertion about that startup tick.

Water needs receive the full negative `WaterLitres` before `sweatTotal *= 0.5`. `ChangingNeedsModelBase.FulfilNeeds` applies that water term only when `TracksThirst`; thirst-rate effects affect `ThirstPoints` separately. The fixed thirst term is still `-5/12 * multiplier` per tick: **10/17.5/22.5 counter-hours per real hour**, independent of sweat volume. These counters are not litres and also have ordinary game-time consumption. `ActiveNoThirst` ignores thirst/hydration; nonliving needs models need not consume the fulfiller. This change does not make those models represent total evaporative loss.

The remaining half is a potential clothing dose, weighted by each worn location's relative hit chance over all bodyparts; only the first item at each wear location is exposed from underneath. Uncovered locations receive no clothing dose. Actual retention depends on coverage, absorbency and downstream liquid exposure. At Human Heavy, 0.48 L/hour is withdrawn and at most 0.24 of that numerical volume is offered across worn locations at default native fluid scale. The consumer passes the number to `LiquidMixture` without a native-unit conversion; nondefault fluid-scale wetting is an existing runtime limitation, not corrected here. There is no new body-surface heat or water simulation in this patch.

Old Human 0.8 meant 19.2/33.6/43.2 L/hour. The new Human 0.02 means 0.48/0.84/1.08. This is an authored moderate-exercise default, not an indiscriminate division of every species by 60 and not a claim to a universal physiological maximum.

## Evidence and adaptation policy

All exact seed numbers below are **game adaptations**, not measurements of every breed, weather condition or fictional species. Sources constrain scale and cooling mechanism. Local skin fluxes must not be called whole-body litres without surface integration; the seed model has no regional or continuous heat response. Approximate sweat density is treated as 1 kg/L for selecting order of magnitude.

- **H: Human exercise anchor.** [Barnes et al. 2019, 1,303 athletes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31230518/) measured sport-specific whole-body means about 0.83-1.51 L/hour, including endurance 1.28 +/- 0.57. The selected 0.48-1.08 progression covers moderate work through harder work, deliberately below extreme heat/elite maxima. It cannot model all subjects.
- **E: Equids.** [Geor et al. 1998, exercise/heat experiment](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jappl.1998.84.6.2042) describes primary cutaneous cooling and losses exceeding 10-12 L/hour. Horse 4.8-10.8 is a working range. [Robertshaw & Taylor 1969](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5347721/) demonstrated heat- and exercise-induced sweating in donkeys. Donkey 1.92-4.32 and Mule 3.36-7.56 are deliberately smaller authored working-animal defaults; they are not reported experimental totals.
- **B: Bovids.** [Pereira et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25436956/) measured breed-dependent skin fluxes around 110-156 g/m2/hour under severe heat and differing respiratory contributions. Cow/Ox 0.48-1.08 L/hour is a rounded whole-animal game envelope, not a conversion from one local measurement. Bison 0.36-0.81 is a conservative related-bovid adaptation. Seed Buffalo is treated as water buffalo: [Lan et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36295811/) found heat responses in animals with low sweat-gland density; 0.12-0.27 deliberately keeps sweating subordinate to other cooling.
- **R: Small ruminants and related ungulates.** [Kasa et al. 1999, treadmill goats](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10590794/) measured regional peaks around 67-80 g/m2/hour. Goat and Sheep 0.072-0.162 are conservative whole-animal defaults; Sheep is a related-ruminant adaptation, not a copied goat measurement. Deer/Reindeer 0.12-0.27, Moose/Elk 0.24-0.54, Giraffe 0.36-0.81, Llama 0.12-0.27 and Alpaca 0.072-0.162 are **low-confidence comparative adaptations**, not recovered species totals. Their exact values are intentionally modest; local glands do not establish human-like sweating. Fur, panting, behaviour and seasonal variation remain unmodelled.
- **C: Camel.** [Abdoun et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22776794/) measured circadian sweating variation and thermal windows. 0.6-1.35 is a conservative fixed exertion approximation; it does not claim constant heat-triggered sweating or reproduce water conservation, heat storage or delayed sweating.
- **P: Other nonzero mammals.** [Gisolfi et al. 1982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6749777/) found marked differences in primate gland activity; Macaque gets 0.024-0.054, not a human per-body rate. [Dawson et al. 1974](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4854555/) established kangaroo sweating during exercise rather than environmental heat: Kangaroo gets 0.12-0.27; Wallaby 0.048-0.108 is a smaller macropod adaptation. [Plochocki et al. 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0176327) documents rhinoceros sweat glands, not a whole-animal sweat rate: Rhinocerous (retained seed spelling) 0.24-0.54 is a low-confidence conservative adaptation; Tapir 0.12-0.27 uses a related perissodactyl analogy. Capybara 0.024-0.054 is a minimal semi-aquatic adaptation, not a measured total; water cooling remains dominant in the game's approximation.
- **Z: Omitted whole-body thermoregulatory sweating.** Zero means this mechanism is not used, not necessarily that absolutely no glandular secretion exists. [Goldberg et al. 1981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7280382/) measured respiratory cooling during panting in dogs. [Moyo et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30326713/) documents pig skin/glands; their presence does not warrant human-like output. Local paw secretions in carnivores, weak secretion in pigs, mucus/skin evaporation and saliva are not substituted for sweat. Elephant interdigital glands do exist ([Lamps et al. 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11798294/)); their local secretion does not justify whole-body human scaling. Elephant/Mammoth/Oliphant get zero, with the latter two explicit extinct/fantasy analogies. Hippo protective skin secretion is not modelled as sweat. Remaining mammals with no established useful whole-body sweat budget use zero as an explicit bounded omission; birds, reptiles, amphibians, arthropods, fish and marine mammals retain zero. This does not make them immune to heat or remove all ordinary thirst. Panting, licking, gular flutter, wallowing, vascular heat exchange and habitat choices are **not implemented by these seed values**.

## Inheritance and existing-world preservation

`HumanSeeder` actually creates **Humanoid -> Organic Humanoid -> Human**, each explicitly assigning 0.02 and a sweat liquid. There is no Organic Animal sweat row in this seeder. AnimalSeeder races explicitly store their template's numerical rate; they do not dynamically inherit Human's rate. The legacy `AnimalRaceTemplate.Sweats` flag is kept as a **fluid-asset catalogue flag** so old stock fluid-name handling remains stable; a zero-rate new race gets a null `SweatLiquid` even if its unused legacy fluid asset is generated. That null is necessary because a non-null liquid with zero volume would still incur the fixed thirst-point charge in the current runtime.

Middle-earth rows override their humanoid parent. Mythical rows explicitly override rate and liquid even when they have a humanoid parent. New supernatural living/nonliving rows explicitly override both. Robots explicitly override both irrespective of parent. Conjured Armageddon creature foundations on master copy their fixed native source race (`ArmageddonCatalogueCreatureFoundations`); they deliberately retain that source/custom world's data rather than forcing a Human constant. On a new Human-bound world they consequently copy the corrected Human value. This is separate from the unpublished playable setting roster.

Runtime `Race` loading reads each persisted race's own rate/liquid; parent creation and cloning copy values. Existing descendants do not dynamically acquire this source edit on load. Human's already-seeded fast path, Animal backfill (missing names only), Mythical missing-name install and Robot existing-row path do not rewrite sweat values. Culture's changed assignments are new `Race` initialisers. Supernatural's refresh previously overwrote sweat unconditionally; this change gates only its sweat assignments on `created`, preserving existing rate **and liquid**, including intentional zero or custom fluids. Other pre-existing refresh behaviour is not claimed to preserve every unrelated field.

`SaveNewRace` accepts only an EF Added race and retains explicit zero after insertion. The legacy SQL default 0.8 uses CLR zero as EF's sentinel: an initial zero can otherwise return 0.8. The helper saves the new row, then explicitly updates its sweat property to zero before continuing. It cannot be used on an existing tracked race. The schema default, historical SQL snapshot, runtime builder fallback and migrations remain unchanged. A second-save failure must be treated as a failed seed operation, not a completed repair; zero-rate races already have null sweat liquid. Ordinary seeder transaction/error behaviour still applies.

There is **no live repair, migration, blanket SQL UPDATE or reconciliation of old/custom sweat values**. Existing stock anomalies remain until an owner deliberately reviews them. No database was connected for this task. Armageddon setting integration is separately [documented and pending](Armageddon_Sweat_Rates_Integration.md).
